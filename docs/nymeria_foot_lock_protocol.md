# Nymeria: foot lock from the head pose (standing and foot slip), protocol (proposed 2026-11-20, recorded when committed and before any run)

Names used in this file: the **data check** (section 3: checks and the labels, no detector), the **main experiment** (sections 5 to 7: detectors, held-out metrics, controls) and the **extra step** (the optional domain-gap measurement). Earlier drafts and the amendments below call them Stage 0, Stage 1 and stage 2; see A7.

Documentation only: no detector, metric or training code exists yet. Facts about the data come from `docs/nymeria_data_format.md`. This protocol is pre-registered: the questions, baselines, thresholds and the success rule below are fixed before any number is computed on the data; changes after the first run are listed as dated amendments, never edited in place.

## 1. Question

Egocentric body tracking often shows the body sliding over the floor when the wearer stands, because the legs are not visible, the head pose has small noise, and "standing still" cannot be told from "moving a little". The question here:

> From the head pose alone, with the noise of a real SLAM trajectory, how well can we tell when both feet are planted, and how much foot slip remains when feet are locked on that decision?

The ground truth for the feet is the Xsens body motion of the same wearer, so the result is a measurement on real data, not on simulated head poses.

## 2. Data and what is needed from the earlier work

- Sequences: all Nymeria sequences on disk. All 206 have `body_motion.zip` and `recording_head.zip` (checked 2026-11-20: 202.6 GB and 142.6 GB), so no download is needed. The 7-sequence video subset has no special role; the video is not needed. Selection rule: order the sequences by the SHA-256 of the sequence name; run the clock-offset step (`nymeria_project_check.py --steps fit`) in that order; a sequence enters the cohort when its refined head-to-Xsens correlation is at least 0.95; stop when the cohort has 60 sequences (all that pass, if fewer). Sequences that fail the offset step are counted and listed, never used and never retried with other settings (jeffery, with coarse correlation 0.07 and best scan 0.18, is expected to fail).
- Clock: the refined offsets of `nymeria_project_check.py` (about 1 ms), taken from its report. Offset shifts of +-100 ms are run as a control (section 6).
- The Xsens-to-SLAM world fit is not needed: the detector sees the SLAM head pose only, and the labels come from Xsens feet only. Hand projection accuracy (docs, `nymeria_hand_eval.py`) is a separate measurement and does not gate this one.
- Cohort size caveat: data check reports both_planted, stepping and walking time per sequence and in total before any detector is run. If fewer than 30 sequences pass the offset step, or the total both_planted time is below 60 minutes, the result is labelled indicative.

## 3. Data check: checks before any detector

1. Segment order. The Xsens segment ids used for the feet (right foot 17, right toe 18, left foot 21, left toe 22; knees 16, 20; pelvis 0) are the standard MVN order and have not been verified. Check on every sequence: foot height below knee height below pelvis height on at least 95% of frames, and the left/right segments on the correct side of the pelvis heading. If this fails, stop and report; no label is built on unverified ids.
2. The four `foot_contacts` columns of `xdata.npz` have an unknown meaning (data-format doc, section 9). They are read as a side check only (agreement with the label below, reported), never used as the label.
3. Label prevalence per sequence: share of frames with both feet planted, with one foot planted, with none; time in each.

## 4. Labels (from Xsens, no model)

A foot is stationary when the smoothed (0.1 s box) horizontal speed of its foot and toe segments is below 0.20 m/s, with hysteresis (off at 0.25 m/s) and a minimum run of 3 frames at 30 Hz. These are the constants of the repository's contact labels (`vertex_contact`: v_on 0.2, v_off 0.25, minimum run 3), so the real-data label and the training label mean the same thing. Height is not used: Xsens height can drift and has no floor mesh.

- **both_planted**: both feet stationary. Primary target.
- **stepping**: at least one foot not stationary.
- **hard stratum**: both_planted frames in which the Xsens head moves horizontally faster than 0.05 m/s (leaning, weight shift, looking around). This is the case the question is about.

## 5. Detectors (inputs: SLAM head pose only, 30 Hz)

| Name | What it is | Selection |
|---|---|---|
| B0 | always both_planted | none |
| B1 | head horizontal speed below tau | tau by maximum balanced accuracy on the training sequences |
| B2 | B1 with hysteresis (tau_on, tau_off) and a minimum run | both thresholds on the training sequences |
| M1 | logistic regression on window features (horizontal and vertical head speed, head position spread over 1 s and 2 s, angular speed, energy of the vertical motion in the 1-3 Hz walking band, speed of the last 0.5 s and the next 0.5 s), L2 weight fixed at 1.0, no other tuning | grouped 5-fold over sequences |

Folds: the fold of a sequence is the integer value of the first 8 hex digits of the SHA-256 of its name, modulo 5. Every fitted quantity (tau of B1, tau_on and tau_off of B2, the weights of M1, the operating point) is fitted on the four other folds only.

M1 is allowed to look 0.5 s ahead (offline), and the same lookahead is applied to B1 and B2 so the comparison is fair. A causal variant (no lookahead) is reported as a second row for every detector, because a runtime tracker cannot look ahead.

Optional extra step, not part of the success rule: the repository's trained contact head applied to the Nymeria head pose without retraining, to measure the gap between simulated and real head-pose noise. It is reported as a domain-gap measurement only.

## 6. Metrics and controls

Per held-out sequence (grouped 5-fold; nothing fitted sees the held-out fold):

- balanced accuracy and F1 for both_planted, and ECE of M1's probability (target: at most 0.05, the repository's contact limit was 0.015, reported as the comparison, not as a gate);
- **missed-lock rate**: share of both_planted frames the detector calls stepping. This is the source of skating;
- **false-lock rate**: share of stepping frames the detector calls both_planted. This pins a moving foot;
- **slip speed**: mean true foot horizontal speed over the frames the detector calls both_planted (m/s), the speed a locked foot would be wrongly held against;
- onset and offset timing: median absolute difference between detected and true planted/stepping transitions (ms);
- all of the above again on the hard stratum, and per narrated activity group where a group has at least 60 s.

Controls:

- **Head-pose noise sweep**: add white position noise (sigma 0, 1, 2, 5 cm) and a random yaw wobble (0, 0.5, 1, 2 degrees) to the SLAM head pose before feature extraction, to emulate a tracker worse than Aria's offline closed-loop SLAM. The measured result applies to this SLAM; the sweep is the sensitivity.
- **Clock shift**: shift the labels by -100, -50, 0, +50, +100 ms. A sharp optimum at 0 supports the clock alignment on spatial evidence.
- **Shuffled control**: M1 with labels circularly shifted by 60 s must fall to the prevalence baseline.
- **SLAM noise floor (secondary measurement)**: distribution of SLAM head horizontal speed on frames where both feet are planted and the Xsens head speed is below 0.02 m/s. This is the combined noise of SLAM and Xsens head sway, not SLAM noise alone, and is reported as such.

Statistics: the sequence is the unit. A participant may appear in several sequences (the pseudonyms differ per sequence, and the metadata gives only height and age group), so intervals over sequences are labelled as such and are not intervals over persons. Report the per-sequence values, their median and interquartile range, a bootstrap over sequences (1000 resamples) for the median of each difference, and a within-sequence block bootstrap (10 s blocks) for each sequence's value.

## 7. Success rule (recorded before the first run)

1. Signal check: B1's balanced accuracy is at least 0.60 on at least 80% of the held-out sequences. If not, the head pose alone is reported as insufficient on this data, and no further claim is made.
2. Main comparison, at the operating point where the false-lock rate on the training folds is at most 5%: M1's missed-lock rate is lower than B2's on more than 60% of the held-out sequences, with a median difference of at least 5 percentage points whose bootstrap 95% interval over sequences excludes 0. Both causal and lookahead rows are reported; the rule is applied to the causal rows (the runtime case).
3. If 2 fails, the result is recorded as: a learned detector did not beat the hysteresis speed threshold on this data, with the numbers.
4. No threshold, feature or operating point is changed after seeing a held-out result.

## 8. Limits stated in advance

- Aria's closed-loop SLAM is an offline, high-quality trajectory. A real-time visual-inertial tracker is noisier; the sweep in section 6 is the only evidence about that and is simulated noise.
- Floor-heavy sequences can have little walking. The result says nothing about walking speeds beyond what the cohort contains.
- The label comes from an IMU suit, with its own foot-position error; both_planted is defined from foot speed only, so a foot that creeps slowly counts as stationary.
- One capture campaign and one device; participants may repeat across sequences. This is a measurement, not a benchmark.

- A5, 2026-11-20, before the first main experiment run (`scripts/nymeria_foot_lock_experiment.py`; it was tested only on synthetic sequences with known answers, and no detector has been run on a Nymeria sequence). It fixes what sections 5 to 7 left open; none of these values is tuned on a result.
  1. Input: the cohort and the refined clock offsets of the second data check run (the 60 labelled sequences). The labels are the data check labels; the script checks that its both_planted seconds equal the data check report and stops if not.
  2. Time base: the SLAM head position on a 30 Hz grid in SLAM time; the label of a frame is the label at the nearest Xsens 30 Hz frame at (SLAM time minus offset). A label shift of s seconds delays the labels by s; with a 33 ms grid a shift of 50 ms is applied as the nearest frame.
  3. Features (position `p`, SLAM angular speed `w` smoothed over 0.1 s): horizontal and vertical speed over a 0.2 s baseline; the mean horizontal speed, mean vertical speed and mean angular speed over a 1 s window; the position spread (root of the summed horizontal variances) over 1 s and 2 s; the power of the vertical head position at 1 to 3 Hz in a 2 s Hann window (FFT bins 1.0 to 3.0 Hz); the mean horizontal speed of the last 0.5 s and, in the lookahead row only, of the next 0.5 s. The causal row uses windows that end at the frame. The lookahead row uses the same windows ending 0.5 s (15 frames) later, so both rows use windows of equal length. Every feature enters as log(0.001 + value) and is standardised with the mean and deviation of the training folds.
  4. B1: lock when the 1 s mean horizontal speed is below tau. B2: the same series with hysteresis (lock when below tau_on, release when above tau_off, tau_on <= tau_off) and the same minimum run of 3 frames as the labels. M1: logistic regression, penalty as `C * sum(log loss) + 0.5 * |w|^2` with C = 1.0, bias not penalised, Newton iterations, no class weights. B2 thresholds are searched on a grid of 12 quantiles (5% to 75%) of the training speeds, every pair with tau_on <= tau_off.
  5. Two operating points per detector, both chosen on the training folds only. "ba": maximum balanced accuracy. "fl5": the most permissive setting whose false-lock rate is at most 5% (for B2: the setting with the lowest missed-lock rate among those with false-lock at most 5%; if none exists, the one with the lowest false-lock rate, and the report says so). M1's operating point is a probability threshold chosen on its own training frames. The success rule of section 7 uses "fl5" for item 2 and "ba" for the B1 balanced accuracy of item 1; both are applied to the causal rows, and the lookahead rows are reported next to them.
  6. A rate over fewer than 30 frames of its class (1 s) is not reported for that sequence; sequences with a missing value are left out of that comparison and counted. Missed-lock of "fl5" is compared per sequence as B2 minus M1; M1 is better when this is positive. The bootstrap over sequences has 1000 resamples of the median difference (seed fixed). The within-sequence block bootstrap uses 10 s blocks and 200 resamples, for the missed-lock rate of B2 and M1 at "fl5" only. Timing differences count a transition when a detected transition of the same direction lies within 1.5 s.
  7. Controls: the noise sweep uses four paired settings (position sigma 0, 1, 2, 5 cm with yaw sigma 0, 0.5, 1, 2 degrees); position noise is white per axis at 30 Hz, and the yaw noise is white per frame and enters the angular-speed feature only (as its frame-to-frame rate); it is run on the causal rows for B1, B2 and M1. The clock shift (-100, -50, 0, +50, +100 ms) and the 60 s circular label shift are run on the causal rows for B1 and M1. The SLAM noise floor is the distribution of the 0.2 s baseline horizontal speed on both-planted frames with Xsens head speed below 0.02 m/s.
  8. Reported next to the main result: the same comparison without the sequences listed as `side_check_inconsistent` in data check (A4), and the upright-only versions of missed-lock, false-lock and balanced accuracy (pelvis above 0.80 m), because six sequences have under 5 minutes of upright time.
  9. Not run in this version: results per narrated activity group (the groups are not defined in this protocol and would need a definition first) and the optional extra step (the repository's trained contact head).
  10. Known weakness of the clock-shift control: it only has power if the sequences contain many transitions; a flat curve means no sharp optimum was found, not that the clock is wrong.
- A6, 2026-11-20, before the first main experiment run on Nymeria data, after an independent review of the first version of the main experiment code (synthetic data only; no detector has been run on a Nymeria sequence). The review re-implemented the metrics, the thresholds, the hysteresis, the logistic fit and the success rule and found them correct, and found no leakage from held-out folds into any fitted quantity (it corrupted all held-out sequences and the fitted values of that fold did not change). It found four problems with the definitions of A5, which are changed here, and some smaller ones.
  1. Causality. A5 said the causal windows end at the frame. The code used a centred baseline of +-3 frames for the speeds and a centred 0.1 s smoothing of the angular speed, so the "causal" row used up to 100 ms of later frames and the lookahead row up to 0.6 s. Now the speed of frame i is the displacement from frame i-6 to frame i divided by 0.2 s, the angular speed is smoothed with a trailing 0.1 s box, and the lookahead row is the same set of windows ending 15 frames later. Checked by changing one frame at a time: the causal features use 0 later frames and the lookahead features at most 15. The "next 0.5 s" speed covers frames i+1 to i+15.
  2. B2 grid. The 12-level grid gave B2 a coarser operating point than B1 and M1, which get an exact 5% false-lock threshold; on a simulated cohort this cost B2 about 2.6 percentage points of missed-lock, against a pass margin of 5. B2 now searches 40 quantile levels (1% to 90%) of the training speeds plus the two thresholds of B1, so on the training folds it can always do at least as well as B1. The report prints the folds in which B2 could not reach 5% false-lock on training, and prints the median held-out false-lock of B1, B2 and M1 at "fl5" next to the rule. The rule itself is unchanged and compares missed-lock only; held-out false-lock can differ from 5%, so it must be read together.
  3. Clock shift. A trailing 1 s window is centred about 0.5 s in the past, so the causal row has no reason to peak at a label shift of 0 (its best delay is expected near +0.5 s). The clock-shift control is therefore also run on the lookahead row, whose window is centred on the frame; that row is the one used to judge whether there is an optimum near 0. A flat curve remains uninformative (A5 item 10).
  4. SLAM gaps. A gap of more than 0.1 s between two SLAM samples would have been bridged by linear interpolation. Frames whose bracketing samples are more than 0.1 s apart, and the 75 frames (2.5 s) around them, are left out of training and evaluation. The largest gap and the excluded share per sequence are written to the report.
  5. Smaller changes: the cache key includes the preparation code; the band-power feature uses a floor of 1e-7 instead of 1e-3 (it is a position power in square metres, and the old floor hid head bobs of 1 cm); the position spread is computed from window variances (numerical precision); a threshold can only lie between two different scores (ties); F1 is 0 when no both-planted frame is locked correctly, instead of missing; the 5 point test uses a tolerance of 1e-9; the outcome text is built per row; the main result is written before the controls run.
  6. Known and not changed: ECE is pooled over all held-out frames; the sequence bootstrap covers the "fl5" missed-lock difference only; the hard stratum reports missed-lock only; the models of the "without side_check_inconsistent" comparison are still trained on all sequences and only the summary leaves them out; the timing metric favours detectors that chatter, because B1 has no minimum run; the first six frames of each series underestimate the speed.

## 11. Results of the first run (2026-11-20)

What the numbers say:

- From the head pose alone, at no more than 5% false-lock, about 62% to 65% of the frames in which both feet are planted are locked (causal), and about 72% to 76% with 0.5 s of lookahead. The mean true foot speed over the locked frames is about 0.015 m/s, against 0.201 m/s if every frame were locked.
- The hard stratum (both feet planted while the Xsens head moves above 0.05 m/s) is where speed fails: 68% of those frames are missed by every detector and M1 is not better there (0.688 against 0.683). With lookahead 59% (B1) and 54% (M1). The noise floor explains why: on both-planted frames with the Xsens head speed below 0.02 m/s the SLAM head speed has a median of 0.013 m/s, a 90th percentile of 0.049 and a 99th percentile of 0.204 m/s (combined SLAM and Xsens head sway), a heavy tail.

- Upright-only (pelvis above 0.80 m): the M1 advantage is 2.7 points, M1 lower on 59 of 60 sequences; no different from the all-frame result.

Limits of this result: labels come from the same IMU suit (section 8) and the suit's contact columns agree with them only moderately; 60 sequences from one campaign and one device, with participants possibly repeated, so intervals are over sequences and not over persons; Aria's offline SLAM is cleaner than a real-time tracker (the sweep is the only evidence on this); only a speed-and-window family of detectors was tested. The 60 sequences have now been used for design and evaluation; any new detector built after this result needs sequences that were not used here (137 of the 206 sequences were not tried in the hash order) and a new recorded protocol.
